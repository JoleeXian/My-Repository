One letter. A different role.
Lactose

The sugar naturally found in milk.

Lactase

The enzyme that helps break that sugar down.

Less lactase can mean trouble digesting lactose. Understanding the difference is a good place to start.

01 / UNDERSTAND

Let’s start with your gut.

You deserve information that makes sense. Here are three things to know before making changes.

The process

Your body uses an enzyme.

Lactase helps break lactose into smaller sugars your body can absorb.

The symptoms

Digestion can feel different.

When lactose is not fully digested, it may cause gas, bloating, or diarrhea. Similar symptoms can have other causes, too.

The individual

There is no single tolerance level.

Some people tolerate small amounts of lactose. Others need to limit more. A clinician can help identify the cause of symptoms.

Intolerance and allergy are different. A milk allergy involves the immune system. Lactase supplements do not make dairy safe for someone with a milk allergy.

02 / MAKE SENSE OF SUPPLEMENTS

Helpful, with limits.

Lactase products may help some people digest lactose. They are a tool, and their effects can vary.

What they can do

They add the enzyme that breaks down lactose. Follow the product’s directions for timing and use. Ask a pharmacist or clinician if you are unsure.

What they cannot promise

They do not cure lactose intolerance or guarantee symptom relief. They also do not treat every cause of stomach discomfort.

How do I read the label?

Check the serving size, enzyme activity (often shown in FCC units), directions, and ingredients. Enzyme activity is different from the weight of a tablet.

Does a matching FCC number mean matching results?

A matching label does not guarantee identical performance for every person or situation. Formulation is one factor worth studying; a label alone cannot predict your symptoms.

When should I ask for help?

Talk with a clinician about persistent or unexplained symptoms and before major diet changes. Ask before giving lactase products to a child or using them during pregnancy or breastfeeding.

03 / BEHIND THE PROJECT

A question that started in the lab.

Student research · Jolee L. Xian

Does supplement form affect enzyme activity?

The project compared coated tablets, fast-acting tablets, gummies, and powder after simulated stomach acidity and neutralization, using a glucose assay to measure lactose breakdown.

Based on “Formulation-Dependent Preservation of Lactase Activity Under Simulated Gastrointestinal Conditions.”

Tablet formulations Produced the highest glucose levels in the tested samples.
Gummy formulation Produced intermediate levels.
Powder formulation Produced the lowest levels.

A lab model is not a human trial. These results do not establish which supplement works best for people, prove protection from acid, or rank all products in a category.

04 / NOURISH

Keep the nutrients.
Find your options.

Milk is one source of nutrients, not a requirement for everyone. If you reduce dairy, plan how to replace what it provides.

Calcium

Support your bones.

Options include calcium-set tofu, canned fish with soft bones, and calcium-fortified foods or drinks. Compare labels for amounts.

Vitamin D

Check for fortification.

Some fortified drinks and cereals, eggs, and fatty fish supply vitamin D. Not every plant-based drink is fortified.

Practical choices

Work with what is available.

Lactose-free dairy or lower-lactose foods may be options. Compare cost, availability, protein, and added nutrients when choosing alternatives.

One small step: compare the calcium and vitamin D on two foods you already buy. A dietitian can help you build a plan around your budget and preferences.
